FIFA WORLD CUP HISTORY AND STATISTICS

The FIFA World Cup is the most prestigious football tournament in the world, held every four years.

2022 FIFA World Cup - Qatar
Argentina won the 2022 FIFA World Cup, defeating France 4-2 on penalties after a 3-3 draw in extra time. The final was played at Lusail Iconic Stadium on December 18, 2022. Lionel Messi won the Golden Ball as the best player of the tournament. Kylian Mbappe won the Golden Boot with 8 goals, including a hat-trick in the final. It was Argentina's third World Cup title and Lionel Messi's first.

2018 FIFA World Cup - Russia
France won the 2018 FIFA World Cup, defeating Croatia 4-2 in the final at Luzhniki Stadium in Moscow. Kylian Mbappe became the second teenager after Pele to score in a World Cup final. Luka Modric of Croatia won the Golden Ball award. The Golden Boot was won by Harry Kane of England with 6 goals.

2014 FIFA World Cup - Brazil
Germany won the 2014 FIFA World Cup, defeating Argentina 1-0 in the final through Mario Gotze's goal in extra time. Germany defeated Brazil 7-1 in the semi-final in what became known as the Mineirazo. Thomas Muller won the Golden Boot with 5 goals. Lionel Messi won the Golden Ball despite Argentina losing the final.

2010 FIFA World Cup - South Africa
Spain won their first World Cup in South Africa, defeating Netherlands 1-0 through Andres Iniesta's goal in extra time. It was the first World Cup held on African soil. David Villa won the Golden Boot with 5 goals. Diego Forlan of Uruguay won the Golden Ball award.

2006 FIFA World Cup - Germany
Italy won their fourth World Cup title, defeating France 5-3 on penalties after a 1-1 draw. The tournament is remembered for Zinedine Zidane's headbutt on Marco Materazzi in the final. Miroslav Klose won the Golden Boot with 5 goals. Zidane won the Golden Ball despite being sent off in the final.

ALL TIME WORLD CUP WINNERS
Brazil - 5 titles (1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, 2002)
Germany - 4 titles (1954, 1974, 1990, 2014)
Italy - 4 titles (1934, 1938, 1982, 2006)
Argentina - 3 titles (1978, 1986, 2022)
France - 2 titles (1998, 2018)
Uruguay - 2 titles (1930, 1950)
England - 1 title (1966)
Spain - 1 title (2010)
